01 / Curatorial Statement

In the 1990s, as the World Wide Web became a more familiar part of everyday life, artists began experimenting with the website itself as an artistic medium. Breaking the Rules of the Web brings together three works that challenge the expectation that online interfaces should be clear, efficient, and easy to navigate. JODI disrupts the visual language of the browser with confusing arrangements of text and code-like imagery. Olia Lialina uses clickable frames to transform reading into an active, fragmented experience. Alexei Shulgin takes ordinary HTML form elements—buttons, checkboxes, and input fields—and treats them as material for visual composition. Each artist works with basic features of the early web, but uses them against their usual practical purposes. Together, these works show how net art made interaction, navigation, and even frustration part of the artistic experience. Instead of simply displaying art on a webpage, these artists made the webpage itself the artwork.

JODI (Joan Heemskerk and Dirk Paesmans)

This experimental website uses disorienting arrangements of text, symbols, and code-like visuals to disrupt familiar expectations of web navigation. Rather than helping visitors find information quickly, it makes the interface itself something to question and explore.

02 / 1996 / Hypertext narrative

My Boyfriend Came Back From the War

Olia Lialina

This interactive story unfolds as visitors click through fragments of text and images. The browser window divides into smaller frames, making the act of navigating the page central to the story's pacing and emotional effect.

03 / 1997 / Interface as art

Form Art

Alexei Shulgin

Shulgin turns standard HTML controls—including buttons, checkboxes, and text fields—into playful visual compositions. Elements normally designed to collect information become artistic shapes that invite curiosity and experimentation.
